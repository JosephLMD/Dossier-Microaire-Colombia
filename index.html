01 · Summary

The proposal in one page

We ask to be recognised as MicroAire's exclusive distributor in Colombia for the PAL line, and asks for four forms of support that convert a flat market into a growing one.

What we ask for. A certified service station in Colombia. Payment terms of 180 days. A structured surgeon training programme based in Bogotá. Support for marketing and congress activity, including half the cost of speakers.

What we commit to. USD 526,000 in the coming year with none of the above. That is the floor, and it matches what was purchased last year. With the four requests in place, USD 604,900, which is the floor plus fifteen per cent, compounding at that same rate each year after.

What it is worth to you. Held across a five year agreement, the difference between those two paths is approximately USD 1.45 million in incremental revenue to you. That figure, not our enthusiasm, is the argument.

The one sentence that matters. Every surgeon relationship MicroAire has in Colombia today runs through one person, and that person is the owner of ACT. This proposal does not move the channel. It formalises where the channel already is.

02 · The channel

Why the channel is already ours

The usual risk in changing distributor is losing the surgeons. In Colombia that risk runs the other way.

MicroAire's presence in Colombia has been built by a single person: the owner of ACT. She is the salesperson, the clinical contact and the point of continuity for every surgeon using PAL in this country. She also owns and runs microairecolombia.com, the only Spanish-language home the brand has here.

What changes with this proposal is not who holds those relationships. It is the structure around them.

03 · Service

Where the brand is losing customers: service

Before anything we are asking for, this is the item that is costing MicroAire the most in Colombia, and it is invisible from Charlottesville.

A PAL handpiece is a mechanical instrument and it wears. With no certified maintenance in the country, a unit needing repair leaves Colombia and comes back about six weeks later. That much is a logistics problem. What follows is not.

On an installed system of about COP 116.7 million before VAT, that is roughly COP 20.1 million a year to keep a machine in service, against the COP 5.8 million it should be.

And so surgeons stop servicing it. They wait five or six years instead of maintaining annually. By then the accumulated maintenance is close to 95% of the price of a new machine. When a unit does fail, the emergency repair takes long enough that the equipment cannot be held in regulatory compliance in the meantime.

The conclusion a Colombian surgeon reaches is not that MicroAire is expensive. It is that MicroAire is not viable to own. That sentence is being said in this market right now, and it is the single most damaging thing happening to the brand here.

What we propose to do about it

Building our own certified station is not the fast answer. A new INVIMA authorisation for a repair station takes roughly two years, and standing one up would cost in the region of USD 30,000. So we have taken a different route.

ACT has contracted Nova Tecnica to work for ACT directly. They are already authorised and already service equipment of this class. This is not a loose subcontract that leaves the surgeon between two companies: the relationship, the response time and the accountability sit with ACT.

A risk that argues for exclusivity. Replica units are sold openly in this region, the first of them by a Colombian company. A single accountable distributor with traceable serial numbers and authorised service is the only practical answer, and it protects pricing and clinical reputation at the same time.

The uncomfortable part. That is not a product problem. It is a support problem, and support is the part a manufacturer delegates to its distributor.

Where the brand actually stands

We will not soften this. In Colombia the reputation of the brand has fallen, and it has fallen for reasons that are fixable rather than fundamental. Surgeons who own the equipment talk to surgeons who are considering it, and what they pass on is the maintenance bill and the six week wait. Every one of the four requests in this proposal exists to reverse a specific part of that.

And there is an argument for doing it here first. If a market where the brand has slipped can be turned around with a service arrangement, structured training and real commercial support, that is not only worth having in Colombia. It is a model MicroAire could take to any territory where the same thing is happening quietly. We would rather be the case study than the cautionary tale.

05 · This year

Why this year was flat, and what it tells us

We would rather explain this precisely than let it sit unexplained. It was not price, and it was not the exchange rate.

The Colombian peso appreciated sharply. The representative market rate moved from COP 4,040.85 in July 2025 to COP 3,361.62 in July 2026, a fall in the dollar of about 17%. In peso terms a USD 20,000 console went from roughly COP 80.8 million to COP 67.2 million. Your equipment became cheaper here, not more expensive.

Two things changed, and neither was price.

Money became expensive. Banco de la República raised its policy rate from 9.25% at the start of 2026 to 12.00% in June, a rise of 275 basis points in a single year, and held it there on 5 August. Annual inflation stood at 5.8% in May.

And the country spent the year waiting. The presidential contest was not settled until the run off of 21 June, and it was decided by under one point, 49.66% against 48.70%, a margin of roughly 250,800 votes. The new government took office on 7 August 2026. Through almost the entire commercial year, capital sat still. In a market where a large share of aesthetic patients come from abroad and their money stays in offshore accounts until the picture is clear, that is not background noise. It is the difference between a surgeon committing to a console this quarter or waiting to see who won.

So the constraint was never price. The equipment became more affordable in pesos while the cost of money rose and the country held its breath. Commercial terms are what let a distributor keep selling through a year like that, and MicroAire does not currently offer them. That is why the request for 180 day terms is not a concession we are seeking. It is the mechanism that keeps the channel moving when conditions do what they did this year.

06 · The requests

The four requests

Each is tied to a mechanism rather than to goodwill, and each has a stated effect on the number in section 07.

A certified service station in Colombia
Repairs in three to five days rather than six weeks. Removes the most frequently cited reason surgeons decline to renew, and removes international repair shipping from your costs.
Payment terms of 180 days
So that ACT can carry the commercial terms its customers need in a market where the cost of money has risen sharply. Separately, where we are able to settle an invoice immediately, we would like to agree a prompt payment discount, which improves your cash position rather than burdening it.
A structured training programme
Based in Bogotá, with local and international faculty, plus sponsored sessions at universities with plastic surgery specialisations. One-to-one access converts better than congress footfall and it seeds the next generation of users.
Speakers at the congresses you already attend
At SAAPS and TDAPS MicroAire covers half the stand, around USD 12,000, and ACT builds, brands and staffs the whole thing. That part works and we are not asking to change it. What is missing is speakers. Both meetings draw surgeons from Central America, the United States, Europe and Asia, so a surgeon presenting PAL from that platform reaches far beyond Colombia, and that is worth more to the brand than it is to us. We are asking MicroAire to fund speakers at the congresses where ACT is present.

08 · The plan

How the units get sold

Five mechanisms. Each names who it targets and who pays for it.

The market we are addressing

The Sociedad Colombiana de Cirugía Plástica publishes a directory of certified plastic surgeons; our count of it stands at approximately 950. Of those, 350 already use MicroAire, which is our installed base. Of the remaining 600, around 20% work primarily in facial and reconstructive surgery and are not candidates for PAL. That leaves an addressable target of 480 surgeons.

We should be straightforward about sequencing: the university programme will not sell a console this year. It is there because the surgeons who buy in 2030 are in a lecture theatre in 2026, and because nobody else is talking to them about MicroAire.

Why the year one target is conservative rather than modest

It matters that none of these mechanisms exists today. The 24 units sold each year come from direct selling alone. There is no replacement programme, no university programme, no financing offer.

The replacement programme is the clearest example. Against an installed base of 350 on a three year upgrade cycle, its capacity is roughly 23 units a year. The year one target assumes only 4 of those 23 convert, which is 17% of what the programme could carry. That is deliberate: a programme in its first year does not run at capacity.

It also answers the obvious question about the years that follow. The growth from year two onward is not an assertion; it is the remaining 19 units a year of capacity that the first year does not touch.

09 · 2027

The SYNERGY 7020 and what 2027 requires

Everything in this proposal points at one thing: 2027 is the consolidation of the new SYNERGY 7020, and consolidation does not happen quietly.

A new console is not sold by having it in a catalogue. It is sold by surgeons seeing it used, by other surgeons they respect talking about it, and by enough repetition that it stops being the new thing and becomes the obvious thing. That is what the requests in section 07 are for, and it is why the timing matters more than usual.

10 · Portfolio

One distributor for the whole portfolio

PAL is the business today. Endotine is what makes ACT an integral distributor rather than a single-line reseller.

PAL, selling now. Power assisted lipoplasty and the cannula family. Registration sits with MicroAire, as it should: the manufacturer owns its registrations and chooses who carries the brand. What we bring is the other half of that. ACT has the team, the experience and the working knowledge of how Colombian registration actually operates, including how to move it, and we commit to keeping everything regulatory relating to MicroAire compliant with local rules. We are asking for the representation to be transferred to ACT.

Endotine, registration in progress. Absorbable fixation for facial rejuvenation. Its INVIMA process is already under way at ACT's own cost and risk, ahead of any agreement between us.

Why this matters to MicroAire and not only to us. The surgeon who buys a PAL console for the body is the same surgeon who lifts a face. One distributor covering both means one relationship, one training calendar, one service channel and one forecast, instead of two partners competing for the same appointment. Endotine is additive: nothing in this proposal makes PAL wait for it.
